CosmoParticleUtils

CosmoParticleUtils provides typical functions needed for particles in cosmological simulations: half-mass radii, centers of mass and of velocity, and smoothing lengths. The functions work with position and velocity matrices, with Unitful quantities, and with the particles of CosmoParticles.

Installation

This package is available through the public registry CosmoAnalysisRegistry. Add the registry once per Julia installation, together with the General registry, which provides the dependencies:

using Pkg
Pkg.Registry.add("General")
Pkg.Registry.add(url="https://github.com/lucasvalenzuela/CosmoAnalysisRegistry")

The package requires Julia 1.12 or newer and can then be installed with:

Pkg.add("CosmoParticleUtils")

Usage

using CosmoParticleUtils, CosmoParticles

# positions and velocities as 3×N matrices
n = 10_000
pos = 100 .* rand(3, n)
vel = 200 .* randn(3, n)
p = Particles(:dm, :pos => pos, :vel => vel, :mass => rand(n))

com = center_of_mass_iterative(p, 50.0)         # shrinking sphere starting at the origin
cov = center_of_velocity(p; rmax=20.0, r0=com)  # mean velocity within 20 around the center
rhalf = half_mass_radius(p; r0=com, rmax=30.0)  # half-mass radius within 30 around the center

# distance of each particle to its 32nd-nearest neighbor in the periodic box from 0 to 100
boxcorners = ([0.0, 0.0, 0.0], [100.0, 100.0, 100.0])
hsml = smoothing_lengths(p, 32; periodic=true, boxcorners)

Reference

CosmoParticleUtils.center_of_mass_iterative — Method
center_of_mass_iterative(pos::AbstractMatrix{<:Number}, mass, r_start::Number; kwargs...)
center_of_mass_iterative(p::AbstractParticles, r_start::Number; posprop=:pos, massprop=:mass, kwargs...)

Returns the center of mass by applying the shrinking sphere method (Power et al. 2003).

The particles are given by their positions pos, a $d × N$ matrix for any dimension $d$, or as AbstractParticles with the properties posprop and massprop. The masses are a vector, or a number, a Fill vector or nothing for equal masses.

The sphere starts centered at r0 with a radius of r_start and moves to the center of mass of the particles within it, while it shrinks by shrinking_factor every iteration. The iteration is stopped when the sphere contains fewer than limit_number and fewer than limit_fraction times the number of particles in the starting sphere, whichever is smaller. In addition, the iteration is stopped when the radius of the sphere drops below limit_radius, which has the same unit as r_start. Throws an error if the starting sphere contains no particles.

The radius of the sphere in iteration step $i$ is $r_\mathrm{start} (1 - f_\mathrm{shrink})^i$. The center is returned as a vector of floating-point numbers. center_of_mass_iterative_info also returns the final radius, the number of particles, and why the iteration stopped.

Keyword arguments

  • r0::Union{AbstractVector{<:Number},Nothing}=nothing: center of the starting sphere; nothing means it uses the origin
  • shrinking_factor::Real=0.025
  • limit_fraction::Real=0.01
  • limit_number::Integer=1000
  • limit_radius::Number=0

Examples

julia> using FillArrays

julia> pos = [4.0 4.2 3.8 4.0 4.0 -6.0 0.0 7.0; 3.0 3.0 3.0 3.2 2.8 0.0 -5.0 -6.0];

julia> center_of_mass_iterative(pos, Fill(1.0, 8), 10.0)
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 4.0
 3.0

CosmoParticleUtils.center_of_mass_iterative_info — Method
center_of_mass_iterative_info(pos::AbstractMatrix{<:Number}, mass, r_start::Number; kwargs...)
center_of_mass_iterative_info(p::AbstractParticles, r_start::Number; posprop=:pos, massprop=:mass, kwargs...)

Returns the center of mass of center_of_mass_iterative together with information on the iteration, as a named tuple with the fields

  • center: the center of mass
  • radius: the radius of the last sphere, whose particles determine the center
  • n: the number of particles in that sphere
  • iterations: the number of times the center was updated
  • reason: why the iteration stopped: :limit_number if the next sphere contains too few particles, :limit_radius if its radius is below the limit, or :single_particle if the last sphere contains a single particle

The arguments and keyword arguments are the same as for center_of_mass_iterative.

Examples

julia> using FillArrays

julia> pos = [4.0 4.2 3.8 4.0 4.0 -6.0 0.0 7.0; 3.0 3.0 3.0 3.2 2.8 0.0 -5.0 -6.0];

julia> mass = Fill(1.0, 8);

julia> info = center_of_mass_iterative_info(pos, mass, 10.0);

julia> info.center
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 4.0
 3.0

julia> info.n, info.reason
(1, :single_particle)

julia> info = center_of_mass_iterative_info(pos, mass, 10.0; limit_fraction=0.5);

julia> info.n, info.reason
(5, :limit_number)

CosmoParticleUtils.center_of_velocity — Method
center_of_velocity(vel::AbstractMatrix{<:Number}, mass; p=0.9)
center_of_velocity(pos::AbstractMatrix{<:Number}, vel::AbstractMatrix{<:Number}, mass; kwargs...)
center_of_velocity(particles::AbstractParticles; posprop=:pos, velprop=:vel, massprop=:mass, kwargs...)

Returns the mass-weighted mean velocity vector of the particles within radius rmax around r0, or of all particles if rmax is nothing.

Only the particles whose velocities deviate least from the median velocity are included: those for which the absolute velocity relative to the median velocity is at most the pth quantile of these magnitudes. The median and the quantile are not weighted, so p is a fraction of the particles and not of the mass: a single massive particle with a deviating velocity, such as a low-resolution particle in a zoom simulation, cannot make up the excluded fraction by itself, and particles of substructures with deviating velocities are excluded as well.

The particles are given by their velocities vel and positions pos, $d × N$ matrices for any dimension $d$, or as AbstractParticles with the properties posprop, velprop and massprop. The positions are only needed if rmax is given. The masses are a vector, or a number, a Fill vector or nothing for equal masses. Throws an error if there are no particles.

Keyword arguments

  • rmax::Union{Number,Nothing}=nothing: radius within which the particles are considered
  • r0::Union{AbstractVector{<:Number},Nothing}=nothing: center of the sphere; nothing means it uses the origin. Only used with rmax.
  • p::Real=0.9: fraction of the particles that are included, between 0 and 1

Examples

julia> using FillArrays

julia> vel = [1.0 2.0 3.0 2.0 50.0; 0.0 1.0 0.0 -1.0 0.0];

julia> mass = Fill(1.0, 5);

julia> center_of_velocity(vel, mass)
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 2.0
 0.0

julia> center_of_velocity(vel, mass; p=1.0)
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 11.6
  0.0

julia> pos = [0.5 1.0 -1.0 0.0 8.0; 0.0 0.5 0.0 -1.0 0.0];

julia> center_of_velocity(pos, vel, [2.0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0]; rmax=2.0)
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 1.8
 0.0

CosmoParticleUtils.half_mass_radius — Function
half_mass_radius(pos::AbstractMatrix{<:Number}, mass=nothing; kwargs...)
half_mass_radius(r²::AbstractVector{<:Number}, mass=nothing; kwargs...)
half_mass_radius(p::AbstractParticles; posprop=:pos, massprop=:mass, r2prop=nothing, kwargs...)

Returns the half-mass radius of the particles around r0 within radius rmax, or of all particles if rmax is nothing.

The particles are given by their positions pos, a $d × N$ matrix for any dimension $d$, by their squared radii r², or as AbstractParticles with the properties posprop and massprop, or r2prop for squared radii. The masses are a vector, or a number, a Fill vector or nothing for equal masses. Throws an error if there are no particles, if a radius is NaN, or if the total mass is not positive and finite.

Keyword arguments

  • r0::Union{AbstractVector{<:Number},Nothing}=nothing: center from which the radii are measured; nothing means it uses the origin. Only for positions, not for r² or r2prop.
  • rmax::Union{Number,Nothing}=nothing: radius within which the particles are considered
  • sortalg=nothing: sorting algorithm passed to usortperm as alg to sort the particles by radius, e.g. QuickSort or a multithreaded algorithm. With nothing, the particles around half the mass are selected without sorting all particles, which is 1.5–4 times faster than single-threaded sorting for 10⁴–10⁷ particles.
  • sorted=false: whether r² is sorted in increasing order, which skips the sorting

Examples

julia> using CosmoParticles, FillArrays

julia> pos = [1.0 0.0 -2.0 0.0; 0.0 3.0 0.0 -4.0];

julia> mass = [4.0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0];

julia> half_mass_radius(pos, mass)
1.6

julia> half_mass_radius(pos, Fill(1.0, 4))
2.5

julia> half_mass_radius(pos, mass; rmax=2.5)
1.2

julia> r0 = [1.0, 2.0];

julia> half_mass_radius(pos .+ r0, mass; r0)
1.6

julia> r² = colnorm2(pos);

julia> half_mass_radius(r², mass)
1.6

julia> p = Particles(:dm, :pos => pos, :mass => mass);

julia> half_mass_radius(p)
1.6

CosmoParticleUtils.smoothing_lengths — Method
smoothing_lengths(pos::AbstractMatrix, k::Integer; kwargs...)
smoothing_lengths(p::AbstractParticles, k::Integer; posprop=:pos, kwargs...)

Returns the distance of each particle to its kth-nearest neighbor, not counting the particle itself.

The particles are given by their positions pos, a $d × N$ matrix for any dimension $d$, or as AbstractParticles with the property posprop. The distances are determined with NearestNeighbors.jl using a KDTree, which is wrapped in a PeriodicTree for periodic boundaries. The particles are distributed over all available threads.

For periodic boundaries, either boxlengths or boxcorners has to be given. With boxlengths, the positions are wrapped into the box from zero to boxlengths, which needs a copy of the positions. With boxcorners, no copy is made, and all positions have to lie within the box. The box values are ignored if periodic is false.

Keyword arguments

  • periodic=false: whether to take the periodic boundaries of the simulation box into account
  • boxlengths=nothing: vector of the box length in each direction
  • boxcorners=nothing: tuple (lowerleft, upperright) of the vectors of the lower and upper box bounds in each direction

Examples

julia> pos = [0.0 1.0 3.0 6.0; 0.0 0.0 0.0 0.0];

julia> smoothing_lengths(pos, 2)
4-element Vector{Float64}:
 3.0
 2.0
 3.0
 5.0

julia> boxlengths = [8.0, 8.0];

julia> smoothing_lengths(pos, 2; periodic=true, boxlengths)
4-element Vector{Float64}:
 2.0
 2.0
 3.0
 3.0

julia> smoothing_lengths(pos, 2; periodic=true, boxcorners=(zero(boxlengths), boxlengths))
4-element Vector{Float64}:
 2.0
 2.0
 3.0
 3.0
